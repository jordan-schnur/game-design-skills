# T10 Against plain Claude

Three pairs of fresh Sonnet agents in copies of the `rogue` fixture: the suite agent had the skills installed and reached `ask-sid` through the real Skill tool; the plain agent was told no skills were available. Suite at commit b567f35. Prompts came from the baseline openers 01-concept, 03-mechanic and 04-playtest, adapted to the fixture (the result files do not record the prompt text, so the prompt column is inferred from the answers; the mechanic and playtest openers were not the baseline's platformer and level-3 cases).

Measures, as PLAN.md defines them: factual errors (named games or facts wrong or unverifiable, stated as fact), unsupported decisions (a verdict or recommendation with neither a test nor an evidence line), bible entry offered or written. A hedged claim ("probably", "from memory") is not counted as stated as fact.

| Pair | Prompt | Factual errors plain / suite | Unsupported decisions plain / suite | Bible entry plain / suite | Words plain / suite |

| X1 | where to start: deckbuilder with a card-growing farm | 0 / 0: plain names seven real games with fair one-line descriptions, hedged where uncertain | 4 / 0: plain decides per-run time, farm feeds the card pool not power, combat first, Godot as default, none with a test or evidence; suite's one verdict has Test and Evidence lines | none / none: suite hands off to /game-bible and ignores the fixture bible as belonging to another game, offers no entry | 903 / 225 |

What the suite changed, and what it cost. Neither side made a factual error: plain Claude named only real games and hedged what it was unsure of, so the suite's gain in these three pairs is not accuracy. The gain is grounding and shape. The suite read the fixture bible and anchored each verdict to it (the Decided pillar "Every pick is a trade", the Open question on decision versus tax, the Planned experiment with Observed pending, the Act 2 "elite fights punish greedy decks" line), while both plain answers to X2 and X3 said they had not seen the game and reasoned generically although the same bible sat in their working copy. Every suite verdict carried a single test with a reversal condition and a Confidence line naming what the panel had not seen; plain answers scattered tests across checklists and left several recommendations untested (six unsupported across the three). On the third measure the suite did nothing: in quick form it offered no bible entry in any pair, and only X1 pointed at `/game-bible` as a hand-off, so the baseline finding "no persistent artifact" is only addressed when the user follows the hand-off. Length moved the other way from what a skill usually costs: suite answers were 173 to 240 words against 440 to 903, about a quarter to two fifths of plain. What was lost in that cut is breadth of diagnosis: X3-plain lists six causes to rule out and X2-plain four readings of the tester's request, where the suite names one test and one hand-off. For a user who wants the checklist, plain is more useful; for one who wants a verdict tied to their own bible and a next step, the suite is.
