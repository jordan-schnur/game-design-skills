# Launch mode

The parts of a `/game-release` launch-mode reply, in order, after the short form (output order in `ask-sid/SKILL.md`, section Answer forms). Every fact about another game (its price, hours, demo, launch), in either mode, carries one of three marks: known (the user said it or it was read this session), from memory (recalled, unverified), or "none I can verify" with an offer to search. The reply works for a developer with one to five friends as testers and no telemetry.

1. **Promise.** One sentence from the bible's north star that store page, trailer, and demo all deliver. An unclear hook makes the launch problem a concept problem: say so and name `/game-bible` to define the north star.
2. **Audience and channels.** The target player from the bible, the games they play now, and the communities, showcases, and creators that serve them, each named. A channel is a place the user can post to, not a medium ("social media").
3. **Analog launches.** Two or three comparable games. For each: price, whether it ran a demo, time on the store page before release, what its launch looked like. Each fact carries its mark; a game with nothing verifiable is listed as "none I can verify" with the search offered.
4. **Demo and trailer.** What the first ten minutes show so the core fun delivers the promise on its own. Each feature shown traces to a pillar; a side feature shown because it looks good is the mismatch to name.
5. **Pricing.** A range from the marked analog prices and the game's length, with the uncertainty stated and the one fact that would narrow it. When no analog price is known or verifiable, ask for two or three games the user considers comparable; until those come, the range is given only as "your guess, unverified" or deferred as `TBD: ask`.
6. **Timeline.** Page up, demo, showcase windows, release window, launch discount. Windows and dates carry their marks. The ship-now vs wait trade-off in one sentence: what shipping now forgoes (wishlists, a showcase) against what waiting costs (hours, the polish tail, goodwill with testers).
7. **Retention and monetization.** Each proposed mechanic in verdict shape (Verdict, Pillar check, Test) against the pillars and the target player. A mechanic from another business model (daily logins, a battle pass, streaks in a finite premium game) fails the pillar check by default; the reply names the worry behind the request (length, replay, reviews) and what that worry calls for instead. P19's When breaking it helped line applies when retention is the business model and the audience signed up for it.
8. **Post-launch loop.** What to collect without telemetry: reviews mined for recurring complaints, refund timing from the store dashboard, asking players where they stopped. What the first patch likely holds. The hand-off line (`SKILL.md`, section Hand-offs) names the first timeline item and `/game-playtest` with the reports, which turns them into one-variable experiments.

Then the Record and Hand-offs sections of `SKILL.md`: one Decision entry offered per price, window, or mechanic verdict, `Proposed`, shown in full.
