# Genres

One section per genre. Each carries the same six fields in the same order: who on the panel leads, what the prototype gate is, what counts as a playtest signal, what a balance target is, how a player's words weigh against what they did, and which catalog entries apply through their Counterexamples line instead of their Explanation. Catalog ids `P01` to `P20` are the twenty entries of `PRINCIPLES.md` next to this file, in order.

**Picking a section.** Read the first line under each heading (the games it covers) and take the one the user's game matches. Name the section in the answer. A game that matches two sections (an action roguelike) names both and takes every field, counterexample entries included, from the section the question falls under. A game that matches none: say so and reason from `PRINCIPLES.md` with no genre lens.

**Citing a counterexample entry.** Name the id and say the Counterexamples line is the one that applies, so the reader does not look up the entry and find the opposite advice.

**Adding a section.** Copy a heading and its six bold fields in the same order, give the first line the description a skill matches against, and keep the section under 25 lines.

## Systems, strategy, roguelike

Games where choosing between options is the experience: 4X and strategy, roguelikes and roguelites, management and automation, deckbuilders, tactics, systemic RPGs.

**Leads:** Meier (chair; decisions, systems, cutting), Wube (telemetry on where runs stall), Larian (systems iterated and documented in the open).
**Prototype gate:** A decision that changes a run. Pass: testers facing the same situation choose differently and can say what they weighed (P02, P06). Fail: everyone picks the same option, or picks without pausing.
**Playtest signals:** Repeated strategies (the same line chosen run after run, P11) and run-length certainty (how early the outcome stops being in doubt, P10). Record the option chosen and the turn or minute at which the run was decided.
**Balance target:** N viable lines: several strategies in use among skilled players with comparable success (P11). State the N before tuning; fewer in use is the fail.
**Testimony weight:** Tiebreaker. Behavior first (P16); a player's account of why they chose decides between two readings of the same behavior.
**Counterexample entries:** P10 (a bounded run may snowball by design), P13 (a dense system may need a reference beside play).

## Feel, action, platformer

Games where moment-to-moment input is the experience: platformers, action and character-action games, shooters, racing, anything where a jump or a hit must feel right before anything else matters.

**Leads:** Miyamoto (the core action, tuned by hand), Miyazaki (challenge calibrated from where players die), Supergiant (feedback to the player; the theme-first exception).
**Prototype gate:** The core action replayed in isolation: one verb, one obstacle, placeholder art. Pass: testers replay it unprompted (P15) and reported feel moves when a parameter moves (P04). Fail: the action is tolerated, not sought.
**Playtest signals:** Death and quit positions, retry counts per section (P12). Many deaths ending in success are a ramp; many deaths ending in quitting are a wall.
**Balance target:** A difficulty ramp with relief: each demand taught before it is tested, a safe room after each peak (P12). A spike is a demand with no prior teaching.
**Testimony weight:** Tiebreaker. Positions and retries first (P16); a player's words decide between frustration and confusion when the counts alone cannot.
**Counterexample entries:** P09 (hand-placed levels carry the arc; emergence is not the goal), P11 (speedrunners' optimization is the pleasure; leave it), P14 (juice that hides state is too much), P15 (theme may lead when theme is a pillar).

## Minimalist systems

Games built from one small rule set that grows its own pressure as a session runs, with no content added: Mini Metro and its kin, with one screen, few verbs, and a failure the player can read.

**Leads:** Meier (what decision each addition creates), Blow (one idea, restraint, rules that teach themselves), Wube (where sessions end).
**Prototype gate:** One rule set producing growing pressure and readable failure with no content added. Pass: a session gets harder on its own, and the player who loses can say what overwhelmed them (P13, P14). Fail: pressure needs new content to grow, or losses feel arbitrary.
**Playtest signals:** Session length before overwhelm (minutes to the first loss, and how that number moves across sessions) and whether players can say why they lost, in their own words.
**Balance target:** Legible pressure growth: each session harder than the last for a reason the player can see. Restraint is a pillar: every addition earns its place against less, written as the decision it creates (P08) and the pressure it adds.
**Testimony weight:** Equal to behavior. The experience is flow rather than mastery, so the player's account of the feeling is evidence in its own right, not a tiebreaker. Record both; a mismatch between them is the finding.
**Counterexample entries:** P05 (mastery is not the point; pattern-learning stays gentle), P14 (feedback that obscures state breaks the readability the genre depends on).
